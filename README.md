# Accounting Cycle Portfolio
A project from [Harisenin.com](https://www.harisenin.com/) Accounting & Tax Bootcamp. This project demonstrates a complete, end-to-end accounting cycle for a simulated business. It showcases the ability to process raw financial transactions, perform period-end adjustments, and generate presentation-ready financial statements using spreadsheet software. 

# Overview
**PT. Dharma Abadi Enginering** was established on January 2, 2024, as a Joint Venture company by three strategic entities, namely PT. Dharma Automotive, PT. Abadi Makmur Jaya, and PT. Electrical Engineering. The company operates in the automotive component manufacturing sector. This statement covers the first two months of the company's operations, reflecting the dynamics of its early establishment phase. This phase is characterized by large-scale fixed asset investments, inventory supply chain structuring, and a rapid transition from low production capacity to sales optimization.

## The Accounting Workflow Implemented
1. **Journalizing & Posting:** Recording initial business transactions.
2. **Unadjusted Trial Balance:** Verifying that total debits equal total credits.
3. **Period-End Adjustments:** Calculating and applying accruals, deferrals, depreciation, and prepayments.

## Financial Workflows
This repository is structured to reflect the complete accounting cycle. Below is a detailed explanation of each section and workflow:

1. **General-Journal**  
   The foundational data entry point. This section contains the chronological log of all daily financial transactions, detailing dates, account names, debits, and credits before they are categorized.

2. **General-Ledger**  
   The master repository of accounts. It categorizes all transactions from the General Journal into specific individual accounts (e.g., Cash, Accounts Receivable, Inventory) to track their running balances over the accounting period.

3. **Adjusting-Entries**  
   The end-of-period refinements. This includes crucial journal entries made at the end of the month—such as machine depreciation, accrued expenses, and prepaid amortizations—to ensure the financial records comply with the accrual basis of accounting.

4. **Adjusted Trial-Balance**  
   The pre-reporting checkpoint. A summarized worksheet listing all finalized account balances after adjustments have been applied. It acts as a mathematical verification, ensuring that total debits perfectly match total credits before generating financial statements.

5. **Income-Statement**  
   The profitability tracker. This financial report details the company's operational performance, calculating total revenues minus the Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) and operating expenses to reveal the final Net Profit or Loss for the period.

6. **Balance-Sheet**  
   The snapshot of financial health. It reports the company's financial position at a specific point in time, structurally balancing Assets (what the company owns) against Liabilities (what it owes) and Equity (shareholders' capital).

7. **Cashflow-Statement**  
   The liquidity monitor. It breaks down actual cash inflows and outflows, categorizing cash movements into Operating, Investing, and Financing activities to show exactly how cash was generated and utilized.

8. **Statement-of-Changes-in-Equity**  
   The capital movement log. This report tracks all fluctuations in the owners' equity over the period, highlighting initial capital injections, additional paid-in investments, and accumulated retained earnings.

9. **Closing-Entries**  
   The period-end reset. Contains the final journal entries required to zero out temporary accounts (revenues and expenses) and transfer their balances into permanent equity accounts, preparing the books for the next accounting cycle.
